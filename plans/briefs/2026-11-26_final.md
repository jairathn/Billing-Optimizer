# The final stage: every claim worked, then the release

The owner's words (2026-11-23 and 11-24): "Before you finally push to main, work every single claim yourself, tracing it
fully. If you fail (open case that was actually already worked, or money you left on the table, something that was marked
closed but shouldn't have been) catalogue and trace that failure with meticulous detail to figure out why it failed and to
ensure it doesn't fail again." "You'll test every claim, open and closed for the past few months to make sure nothing's
leaking and no claims advertised as open are actually closed? We're looking for 100% accuracy. No lost money, no
unnecessary work."

1. Run the merged code on a fresh world copy (the full 837 history), two cycles (they must agree item for item).
2. Score every claim with services since 2026-01-01, open and closed, with A17 (every claim, not a sample).
3. Hand-trace a fresh stratified sample per verdict class (at least 30 per class, every class), blind to the code's and
   the auditor's verdict first, then compared. Agreement must be 100% on the owner's three failure kinds (already worked
   or paid but listed, owed but not listed, closed wrongly). Every disagreement is catalogued in
   `plans/final_trace_<date>.md` (finding ids, codes, payer groups, dollars, the rule that decided, why it failed: no
   patient data) and fixed with a failing test, then the whole stage runs again.
4. The release gates: the suite in the four environments and the node checks on a clean checkout; the catch-up harness on
   a fresh copy of the live store (plans/tools/catchup.py: every item that closes or leaves explained by a payment, a
   newer copy of its remittance or an heir); the PHI scan of every log, manifest and bundle; the docs (OPERATIONS.md's
   stale lines, the README table, the runbook's ledger backup and restore, .env.example settings); the release note.
5. Push the integration commit to `main` (fast-forward), check `/api/health` on both sites shows it, watch the server's
   first cycle and Vercel's first pressed run (status.json: errors, memory peak), and acknowledge the swing alarm the
   release's own re-analysis raises once the cause is checked.
