# Data the practice has to supply, and why

Ranked by how much it changes what the system can prove.

1. **A current remittance feed.** The newest payment in Dropbox is dated October 9, 2026. Whoever downloads the TriZetto remittance files and uploads them to `Trizetto Data` has stopped, or uploads in batches a month apart. The alerts can only be as current as the files. Who: billing lead.

2. **The remittance files for the weeks that are missing.** A payer that pays every week sent next to nothing in these weeks and then started again, and the visits those weeks would have paid are still short: UnitedHealthcare (payer ID 87726) for December 22, 2025 to January 11, 2026, which leaves about 520 claims for November and December 2025 visits missing (roughly 6 percent of each month); and Anthem (ANTHM) for September 7 to 20 and September 28 to October 4, 2026, which so far leaves about 375 claims for August 2026 visits missing. Either the files for those weeks never reached the folder or the payer paid nothing. Every count on the site is missing those visits, and any downcoding in them. The Data tab keeps the list current ("Remittance weeks that look missing") and drops a week once its files arrive. Who: billing lead, with TriZetto.

3. **837 claim files on an ongoing basis.** TriZetto keeps the submitted 837P files; only a few days of them are in Dropbox (`PROFESSIONAL_Claims_*.txt`). They are the proof of what was billed. With them the system catches the case the billers described, where the remittance shows a level 3 billed and paid and only the claim shows it went out as a level 4. On the days that are present, the submitted code matched the paid code on every line, so that pattern has not appeared yet, but it can only be seen where claim files exist. Who: TriZetto account administrator (claim file retrieval to the same Dropbox folder).

4. **Annual ModMed EMA exports for 2024, 2025 and 2026** (Encounter and Charge Lines; ERA and Payment Line Adjustments; Claim and Claim Status). Only weekly exports from July 27, 2026 onward are in Dropbox. With the annual files every finding shows how EMA handled the shortfall (written off, moved to the patient, ignored), and the breakdowns by location and diagnosis cover the whole history. They also decide what belongs in the work queue. The remittances name the patient on every finding, but they cannot say whether the billers already rebilled, wrote off or appealed it; today EMA's side is known for about one finding in eleven of those still inside a filing window, because the exports cover ten weeks. The Claim and Claim Status and the ERA and Payment Line reports are the two that carry it, and the longest filing window reaches back two years, so they are wanted from November 2024 on and then every week. Add the Patient ID column to the Claim and Claim Status report (the Encounter and Charge Lines report already offers it): a claim EMA shows as rejected or still waiting for payment has no remittance, so without that column nothing in the folder says whose claim it is. Today location is known for recent claims and estimated from each provider's usual site for the rest, and diagnosis is known only for recent claims. Who: practice administrator (EMA report export).

5. **Executed payer contracts and fee schedules with effective dates** (Anthem, Humana, UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, Cigna, UMWA, the larger TPAs). Turns "below the payer's usual rate" into "below contract" with the clause attached, settles the filing window for each payer, and settles whether Anthem commercial visits at 59 to 72 percent of Medicare and the multiple-procedure terms are what was signed. Who: practice administrator.

6. **Payment vendor enrollment status** (Zelis, Optum Pay, Payspan, ECHO, InstaMed) for each payer: virtual card, fee-bearing ACH, or free standard EFT. The fee findings quantify the fees; the enrollment records show what to switch.

7. **Provider roster** (NPI, credential, start and end dates, locations). Replaces the inferred physician and non-physician tiers and the inferred usual site, and cleans up the provider names, which today are taken from the payers' spellings.

8. **Appeal outcomes that never reach a remittance.** A won appeal shows up as a payment and is recorded automatically. A lost appeal does not, so the billers need to mark it "lost" in the dashboard. Without that the overturn rate by payer cannot be computed.

9. **MIPS feedback reports (QPP)** for performance years 2022 to 2024. The Medicare bonus fell from about $308K (2024 payment year) to $82K (2025) to $14K (2026); the reports say which category dropped.

10. **Prior authorization logs** for radiation therapy and biologic injections: authorization denials are frequent and preventable.

11. **Medicare fee schedule files** (CMS RVU files and the Kentucky and Indiana GPCIs for 2024 to 2027). Today the Medicare baseline is inferred from Medicare remittances, which works for codes Medicare pays often. The official files extend it to every code. They matter more in 2027, when Medicare is reported to start paying the lower of a same-day visit and minor procedure at 80 percent (see docs/RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md).

12. **The EMA appointment export** (scheduled, arrived, no-show, cancelled, with clinician, office, appointment type and the time the slot was booked). The Providers tab can count the patients each clinician saw; it cannot tell an empty schedule from patients who did not come, or how far out each clinician is booked. Those decide whether a light schedule is a demand problem, a scheduling problem or a no-show problem. Who: practice administrator (EMA report export, the same Dropbox folder). Nothing on the site reads this export yet; it is the next thing to build once a sample is in the folder.

13. **Encounter and Charge Lines in the full layout, by date of service, for a rolling six weeks each week.** The exports in Dropbox come in two layouts. The thin weekly one has no Bill Creation Date, Bill ID or Patient ID. A visit only appears in an export once its bill exists, so a week pulled on its last day misses every visit billed later. The site reads when each file was pulled from the workbook itself and counts a day only when an export covering it was pulled late enough for that clinician's bills to exist, so a week pulled a month later counts and a week pulled as it ended waits until another export covers it. The full layout, pulled again over six weeks each time, keeps every day covered, names the patient (so self-pay visits are counted as patients, not bills) and is the only layout that shows how long each clinician takes to turn a visit into a bill.

14. **Support staff cost by clinician and pay terms**, if the provider view is to be read against cost. Neither is in any file the practice exports today.

15. **Remittances for VA community care.** The EMA exports show these visits (payer ID VACCN, a few a day across the practice) and no remittance for any of them has ever arrived in the folder, so they are missing from every count built from remittances and from the downcoding checks. Ask TriZetto whether that payer's remittances can be enrolled and delivered like the others.
