# Data the practice has to supply, and why

Ranked by how much it changes what the system can prove. The figures were measured on the archive on November 13, 2026.

1. **A current remittance feed.** The newest payment in Dropbox is dated October 9, 2026. Half of all visits are paid within 19 days and nine in ten within 37, so September's visits are about 60 percent paid in the store and October's almost not at all, and a denial that was corrected and paid after October 9 still shows as open. Whoever downloads the TriZetto remittance files and uploads them to `Trizetto Data` has stopped, or uploads in batches a month apart. The alerts can only be as current as the files. TriZetto can also put the 835 files in an SFTP mailbox that a program collects from (TriZetto support turns it on; the password is reset once a year). Before asking for it, ask TriZetto whether a second mailbox would take the files away from ModMed, which collects the same remittances. Who: billing lead, with TriZetto.

2. **Executed payer contracts and fee schedules with effective dates, Anthem commercial and Humana Medicare Advantage first** (then UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage, Aetna, Cigna, UMWA and the larger TPAs). In 2025 Anthem commercial allowed office services at about 88 percent of what Medicare paid the practice for the same codes (visits at about 73 percent), about $245,000 a year less than Medicare would have paid; Humana Medicare Advantage allowed about 95 percent (about $115,000 a year less) and UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage about 93 percent (about $41,000). The site cannot tell whether those are the signed rates or errors. With the contracts every underpayment finding is measured against the contract instead of the payer's own usual rate (877 of them are still inside a filing window, $69,000), the filing window for each payer comes from the contract, and the multiple-procedure terms can be checked. Who: practice administrator.

3. **The three EMA reports every week in one fixed form, and ModMed's EHI export once.** EMA's side of a finding (written off, moved to the patient, rebilled) is known today for about 3 percent of the findings still inside a filing window, because the ERA and Payment Line Adjustments report covers only late July to August and one week of September. The charge exports also miss visits: of the remittance claims for the weeks of service starting August 17, August 24, August 31 and September 7, an Encounter and Charge Lines export holds 69, 32, 0 and 27 percent. The Data tab lists such weeks ("EMA charge exports that miss visits").
   - **Every week, the last six weeks by date of service:** Encounter and Charge Lines in the full layout (with Patient ID, Bill ID and Bill Creation Date), ERA and Payment Line Adjustments, and Claim and Claim Status with the Patient ID column added. A visit appears in an export only once its bill exists, so a week pulled on its last day misses every visit billed later; repeating six weeks each time keeps every day covered. A claim EMA shows as rejected or still waiting for payment has no remittance, so without Patient ID on the Claim and Claim Status report nothing in the folder says whose claim it is. The site reads these three today.
   - **Once, the all-patient EHI export.** Every certified health record system has to offer it, ModMed charges nothing for the standard export, and it is requested through ModMed support. It holds the billing tables for the whole history in one consistent form: claims and each submission, the link from each claim to its ERA, line adjustments with their reason codes, payments, accounts receivable, and charges with modifiers, units, place of service and diagnoses. Nothing on the site reads it yet (it is a set of delimited files, not the Excel reports above); a reader for it is the next step once a copy is in hand, and until then putting it in the Dropbox folder changes nothing. ModMed's data dictionary for it is confidential, so its contents stay out of this repository.

   Who: practice administrator.

4. **The remittance files for the weeks that are missing.** A payer that pays every week sent next to nothing in these weeks and then started again, and the visits those weeks would have paid are still short: UnitedHealthcare (payer ID 87726) for December 22, 2025 to January 11, 2026, which leaves about 520 claims for November and December 2025 visits missing (roughly 6 percent of each month); and Anthem (ANTHM) for September 7 to 20 and September 28 to October 4, 2026, which so far leaves about 375 claims for August 2026 visits missing. EMA also shows 266 claims for visits from July 27 to October 9 as paid that have no remittance in the folder (126 Blue Cross, 84 Humana Medicare, 23 Medicare Kentucky). Every count on the site is missing those visits. The Data tab keeps the list of missing weeks current ("Remittance weeks that look missing") and drops a week once its files arrive. Who: billing lead, with TriZetto.

5. **Payment vendor enrollment status** (Zelis, Optum Pay, Payspan, ECHO, InstaMed) for each payer: virtual card, fee-bearing ACH, or free standard EFT. The fee findings quantify the fees; the enrollment records show what to switch.

6. **Provider roster** (NPI, credential, start and end dates, locations). Replaces the inferred physician and non-physician tiers and the inferred usual site, and cleans up the provider names, which today are taken from the payers' spellings. About 9 percent of remittance claims name no rendering provider (about $850,000 of payments a year).

7. **Appeal outcomes that never reach a remittance.** A won appeal shows up as a payment and is recorded automatically. A lost appeal does not, so the billers need to mark it "lost" in the dashboard. Without that the overturn rate by payer cannot be computed.

8. **Prior authorization logs** for radiation therapy and biologic injections: authorization denials are frequent and preventable.

9. **The EMA appointment export** (scheduled, arrived, no-show, cancelled, with clinician, office, appointment type and the time the slot was booked). The Providers tab can count the patients each clinician saw; it cannot tell an empty schedule from patients who did not come, or how far out each clinician is booked. With it the site could also list arrived visits that never became a bill. Who: practice administrator (EMA report export, the same Dropbox folder). Nothing on the site reads this export yet; it is the next thing to build once a sample is in the folder.

10. **Support staff cost by clinician and pay terms**, if the provider view is to be read against cost. Neither is in any file the practice exports today.

11. **Remittances for VA community care.** The EMA exports show these visits (payer ID VACCN, a few a day across the practice) and no remittance for any of them has ever arrived in the folder, so they are missing from every count built from remittances and from the downcoding checks. Ask TriZetto whether that payer's remittances can be enrolled and delivered like the others.

12. **The 2026 MIPS payment adjustment file only.** The Medicare bonus fell from about $308,000 (2024) to $82,000 (2025) mostly because CMS ended the additional adjustment for exceptional performance after the 2024 payment year (CMS, 2024 MIPS Payment Year Payment Adjustment User Guide, page 14). In 2025 the practice's Medicare lines carried +2.05 percent against a national maximum of 2.15 percent (CMS, 2025 guide, page 7), so 2025 was close to the best possible. 2026 lines carry +0.66 percent and CMS's 2026 guide does not publish the maximum, so the Payment Adjustment CSV (qpp.cms.gov, Performance Feedback) is the only way to tell whether 2026 left anything on the table. The 2022 to 2024 feedback reports are not needed.

## No longer asked for

- **837 claim files.** A remittance has to report the submitted code whenever the payer paid a different one (X12 interpretation RFI 2375), and the EMA charge export carries the billed code, modifiers, units, diagnosis and provider. For August and September 2026 visits the billed code on the remittance matched the EMA charge on 9,755 of 9,767 lines and on 3,382 of 3,389 E/M lines; the seven are one payer writing the modifier into the code field, and none is a change of level. The week of 837 files in the folder agrees (78 of 79 lines). An 837 would add only what TriZetto changed in transit.
- **CMS fee schedule files** (RVU and GPCI). They are public, so they are not the practice's to supply; the system is to load them from CMS.
